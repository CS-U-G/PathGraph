# Graduation-Project
University students and recent graduates often experience academic and professional disorientation when selecting an appropriate career path. Many learners complete courses or degree programs without having a clear understanding of how their current knowledge and abilities relate to the practical requirements of their desired occupations.
The rapid pace of job market development makes this problem more difficult. New technologies, tools, frameworks, and professional practices continuously change the skills required for roles such as Data Scientist, Full Stack Developer, Cybersecurity Analyst, Artificial Intelligence Engineer, and Cloud Engineer. As a result, students may depend on outdated information, general advice, or unstructured online content when planning their professional development.
A major challenge is identifying the learner's Skill Gap, which represents the difference between the skills the learner currently possesses and those required for a selected career role. Without a reliable skill-gap analysis, learners may study unrelated subjects, overlook important prerequisite skills, or follow learning resources in an unsuitable order.
Although many educational websites offer online courses, most present the same content to all users. They do not sufficiently evaluate the learner's existing skills, relate those skills to a specific career objective, or generate a personalised sequence of learning activities. Learners must therefore search through many platforms and independently decide what they should learn, in what order, and how their progress should be measured.
Educational institutions also face challenges in understanding whether their students possess the competencies required by the labour market. Traditional academic records mainly describe completed subjects and grades, but they do not always show practical skill readiness for specific career roles. Institutions need better tools for identifying common skill gaps, providing academic and career guidance, and improving the alignment between educational programs and employment requirements.
There is therefore a need for a centralised platform that can evaluate each learner's current abilities, compare them against the requirements of a selected career role, identify missing competencies, and generate a structured learning roadmap. The proposed system will address this need through a rule-based web platform that combines structured skills self-assessment, job-role requirement matching against a predefined skills database, a deterministic gap-analysis engine, graph-based knowledge representation of skills and prerequisites, interactive roadmaps, and progress tracking.
The project is intended as a university graduation project that demonstrates the practical integration of procedural, rule-based system design, relational and graph database modelling, web application development, and user-centred interface design.
